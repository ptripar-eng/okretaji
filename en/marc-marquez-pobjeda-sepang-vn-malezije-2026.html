Marc Márquez wins at Sepang and extends his lead: eighth title drawing closer

The Spaniard claimed his seventh win of the season and 80th MotoGP triumph in Malaysia. Pedro Acosta finished second and Alex Márquez third, while Jorge Martín's absence further shifted the title fight landscape.

Okretaji · 1 November 2026 · Reading time: 4 min

Marc Márquez won the Malaysian Grand Prix at the Sepang circuit, taking another huge step towards his eighth world championship title. The Ducati rider completed 20 laps in 40:14.236, finishing 4.172 seconds ahead of KTM's Pedro Acosta. Third place went to his brother Alex Márquez, who started the race from pole position.

He took the initiative from the start

Alex Márquez started from pole position, but Marc reacted better to the lights going out and entered the first turn ahead of his brother. Alex tried to retaliate at turn four, but ran too wide and missed the chance to take the lead. Marc stayed at the front, while Pedro Acosta worked his way through toward the leading duo.

In the early stages of the race, the gap between the brothers was small. Acosta showed he had the pace to fight for the podium, managing to close in with the fastest lap. Still, Márquez did not panic or try to break away immediately. He controlled the race, managed his tyres and gradually extended the gap.

Key moment: Acosta passes Alex, Marc pulls away

Around the halfway point of the race, Acosta attacked Alex Márquez at turn nine and took second place. This gave him a clear track ahead, but Marc already had a big enough advantage not to have to respond to every fast lap. The gap continued to grow and reached over four seconds in the closing stages.

This is perhaps the most important part of Marc's victory. It is not just about raw single-lap pace, but about controlling the rhythm throughout the entire race. Sepang is demanding on tyres, especially in long corners and high temperatures. In such conditions, pushing too hard in the opening laps can take its toll later. Marc managed to maintain his pace without a noticeable drop in performance.

Martin's absence changes the championship

Jorge Martin did not compete in Malaysia after fracturing his wrist in the previous race in Australia. He tried to ride in practice, but abandoned the rest of the weekend. With his win, Márquez extended his lead to 44 points: after Sepang he has 399 points, Martin 355, and Marco Bezzecchi 332.

It is important to emphasise that the title is not yet mathematically secured. The next race takes place in Qatar, where Marc can secure his first match point for the title. According to the current points scenario, a combination of results in Qatar could secure him the championship before the final two race weekends.

Third win in Malaysia, seventh this season

Sepang is a special place for Márquez: this is his third victory in Malaysia in the MotoGP class, following 2014 and 2018. It is also his seventh main race win of this season and the 80th of his MotoGP career. After a difficult period marked by injuries, his return to the top did not happen overnight. This season shows just how competitive he has become again in the title fight.

Okretaji Conclusion: Márquez did not win just because he had the fastest motorcycle. The start, calmness in the duel with his brother, tyre management and the ability to pull away when Acosta took second place made the difference. If he maintains such control in Qatar, the title fight could be resolved before the end of the season.
