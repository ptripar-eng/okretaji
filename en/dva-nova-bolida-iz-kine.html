Two New F1 Cars from China?

Shanghai has hosted Formula 1 races since 2004. Perhaps one day we will watch a Chinese team emerge from the pit lane on that very same circuit. Two names have already come up in discussions with the FIA.

Okretaji · 11 November 2026

If BYD first brings to mind an electric car you saw at the traffic lights, try imagining it for a moment on the other side of the red lights. In Shanghai, with two cars on the starting grid. How would a manufacturer that most people know for batteries even get there?

The question became more interesting on 9 November, when FIA President Mohammed Ben Sulayem, in an interview with The National , spoke about a possible twelfth team. Among the manufacturers that have contacted the FIA, BYD and Geely were mentioned. Ben Sulayem would gladly welcome a Chinese team if it can bring sporting and commercial value to the championship. That is where the story stands for now. Neither a formal application nor a debut date has been publicly confirmed. The National

Whose name would be on the car, and whose power unit would be inside?

For the answer, it is worth looking at Cadillac. The Americans arrived in Formula 1 this year with their own team, but with a Ferrari power unit. General Motors received approval to become a power unit supplier of its own starting in 2029. Cadillac secured a place on the grid after more than a two-year process, with FIA approval and a commercial agreement with Formula 1. FIA – Cadillac entry · FIA – GM power units

Let us imagine then that BYD or Geely takes a similar path. They could build the team and chassis around an existing supplier's power unit, leaving the decision about their own power unit for later. They could also seek a partnership with one of the current teams. In the first case, we would get two new cars on the grid. In the second, a Chinese name could appear on a car we are already watching.

Why would an electric car manufacturer even be interested in F1?

When a driver exits a corner onto the long straight in Shanghai, they have to decide where it pays off the most to spend the electrical energy they harvested during the lap. If they spend it on an attack, how much do they have left for defence? With the 2026 regulations, the electrical part of the power unit can develop up to 350 kW. This makes it an interesting question what a manufacturer with experience in batteries and electric propulsion could bring to Formula 1 if they one day decided to develop their own power unit.
