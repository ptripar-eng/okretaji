Marco Simoncelli and the Honda he never raced

In October 2011, Simoncelli rode the Honda he was supposed to get for the following season for the first time. Two weeks later, in Australia, he achieved the best result of his MotoGP career.

Okretaji · 26 November 2026

Monday, 3 October 2011, Marco Simoncelli was still in Motegi. The day before, he had finished fourth at the Japanese Grand Prix, and now a test of the new Honda awaited him. His RC212V had 800cc, and this one, badged RC213V, had a thousand.

A week earlier, an agreement was reached for 2012. Simoncelli would stay with Fausto Gresini, and Honda would give him a factory motorcycle and HRC support. He was 24 years old and only in his second season in the premier class. Honda had already given him a go on the bike he was supposed to ride for his third.

Two zeros then the title

In Coriano, not far from Rimini, Simoncelli grew up riding minimotos. He was about one metre eighty-three tall, so he looked rather awkward on the small 125cc Aprilia. Beneath his helmet hid the curls he was known for, and his bike was adorned with the number 58.

He took his first world championship victory in 2004 in the rain at Jerez. He started from pole, and took the lead when Casey Stoner crashed three laps from the end. A year later he won at the same track. He then moved up to the 250cc class with Gilera and spent two seasons waiting for his first podium.

In his third he became champion. He finished the first two races of 2008 without points on the older Gilera LE. In Estoril he took pole and finished second, and in Mugello he claimed his first class win. At the start of the final lap, as they battled for the lead, Simoncelli and Héctor Barberá touched on the home straight. Barberá crashed heavily, while Simoncelli stayed on his bike and won. When he received a factory Gilera RSA in the middle of the season, he was already leading the championship. He secured the title with a third place in Sepang on 19 October 2008, a race before the end.

A year later he briefly tried Superbike. In Imola, aboard an Aprilia RSV4, he replaced the injured Shinya Nakano. In race one he crashed. In race two, on the 13th lap, he attacked teammate Max Biaggi, passed him and finished third behind the two factory Ducatis. That was his only weekend in the World Superbike championship.

Speed that made them wait and call him out

Simoncelli finished eighth in his debut MotoGP season. In his second he was already fast enough for the front row, and unpredictable enough for his colleagues to publicly call him out. Jorge Lorenzo confronted him in Estoril over his overtaking style. In Le Mans, Simoncelli collided with Dani Pedrosa while fighting for second place. Pedrosa broke his collarbone, and Simoncelli received a ride-through penalty.

At the next race, in Barcelona, he took his first MotoGP pole. He was 16 thousandths faster than Stoner. In Assen he was once again fastest in qualifying, and he waited until August for his first podium, when he finished third in Brno.

His best race was on 16 October at Phillip Island. Stoner won and secured the title, while Simoncelli finished second, ahead of Andrea Dovizioso and Dani Pedrosa. He had a contract signed for the next season, a tested new bike, and a fresh second place in Australia. The next race was in Sepang.

Number 58

On the second lap of the Malaysian GP, 23 October 2011, Simoncelli was running fourth. He crashed at turn 11, in an accident that involved Colin Edwards and Valentino Rossi. The race was red-flagged. Simoncelli passed away that afternoon.

Four days later in Coriano, two motorcycles stood beside his coffin, the Gilera with which he won the title and the MotoGP Honda. In Valencia, Kevin Schwantz rode his Honda on the parade lap, and a year later the Misano circuit was named after Simoncelli. In 2016, MotoGP retired the number 58 from the premier class.

In Moto3 that number was given a second life. The SIC58 team, founded by his father Paolo, entered the championship in 2017. Two years later in Jerez, at the track where Marco took his first win, its riders finished first and second. Niccolò Antonelli won, with Tatsuki Suzuki arriving right behind him. Fifteen years after Marco's first win, Paolo had reason once again to look up at the highest step of the podium.
