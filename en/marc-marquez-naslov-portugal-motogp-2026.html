Márquez can become champion as early as Saturday: what does he need in Portugal?

Sixty-point lead and two races to go. Here are all the key scenarios in Portimão.

Okretaji · 17 November 2026 · Editorial staff

Marc Márquez arrives in Portimão with a 60-point lead over Jorge Martín and 74 over Marco Bezzecchi. There are 74 points left on the table this season, but Ducati can secure its leading rider an eighth MotoGP title as early as Saturday's sprint race.

Title as early as Saturday?

The simplest calculation for Márquez is a sprint win: that makes him champion regardless of his rivals' results. Second place also secures him the title if Martín finishes third or worse. Even eighth place can be enough if Márquez scores at least two points more than Martín.

Bezzecchi still has a mathematical chance, but only until Márquez scores points on Saturday. Any points finish for the number 93 rider in the sprint eliminates the Italian from the title race.

If the decision waits until Sunday

Márquez must leave Portugal with a lead of at least 37 points over Martín. In other words, over the entire weekend he can afford to lose a maximum of 23 points relative to the Aprilia rider, or a maximum of 36 to Bezzecchi.

In the event of a points tie with Martín, Márquez holds the advantage on main race wins: seven to one. With Bezzecchi the math is trickier: if the Italian wins on Sunday and matches the win count, the final decision could move to Valencia.

What does that mean on track?

Martín has a huge deficit to make up, while Márquez does not need to win at all costs on Sunday. Portimão, known for its elevation changes, rapid transitions and demanding braking zones, is still not a place where an advantage can be considered safe before the chequered flag.

Saturday's sprint on 21 November is the first match point. If the title isn't decided then, Sunday's Portuguese Grand Prix on 22 November brings a new opportunity. The final race of the season takes place in Valencia.
