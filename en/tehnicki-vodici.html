Garaža · Praktični vodiči

Manje nagađanja.
Više sigurne vožnje.

Kratki kontrolni popisi za vozače. Uvijek provjeri priručnik konkretnog vozila: tlakovi, tolerancije, postupci i servisni intervali razlikuju se među modelima.

Sigurnost na prvom mjestu. Ovi vodiči pomažu pri osnovnoj provjeri, ali ne zamjenjuju servisni priručnik ni stručni pregled. Ako su guma, kočnice, upravljanje ili konstrukcijski dio oštećeni, nemoj nastaviti vožnju dok se problem ne procijeni i otkloni.
Prije polaska

1. Brza provjera motocikla u 5 minuta

Jednostavan pregled koji smanjuje šansu da previdiš očit problem prije vožnje.

  • Gume: vidljiva oštećenja, strana tijela, neuobičajeno trošenje i tlak prema priručniku.
  • Kočnice: osjećaj na polugama/pedali, vidljiva curenja i stanje dostupnih dijelova.
  • Lanac i prijenos: podmazivanje, oštećene karike i napetost prema specifikaciji modela.
  • Tekućine i svjetla: vidljiva curenja, razine prema uputama, svjetla i pokazivači smjera.
  • Kontrole: gas se slobodno vraća, upravljač se kreće bez zapinjanja, ogledala su podešena.

Stani ako: primijetiš curenje kočione tekućine, pukotinu, oštećenje gume, zapinjanje upravljanja ili sumnju u kočnice.

Gume

2. Kako provjeriti tlak u gumama

  • Koristi pouzdan manometar i mjeri kad su gume hladne, prema uputama proizvođača.
  • Prati vrijednosti iz priručnika ili naljepnice vozila, uključujući preporuke za suvozača/prtljagu.
  • Ne koristi maksimalni tlak otisnut na bočnici kao automatsku preporučenu vrijednost za cestu.
  • Provjeri i dubinu/ujednačenost profila, pukotine, izbočine i zabijene predmete.

Nemoj: ispuštati zrak iz zagrijane gume samo zato što je izmjereni tlak porastao tijekom vožnje.

Pogonski sklop

3. Njega lanca motocikla

  • Provjeri napetost na mjestu i u položaju koje propisuje priručnik; vrijednosti nisu iste za sve modele.
  • Pregledaj karike, zupčanike i eventualne krute ili oštećene dijelove.
  • Koristi sredstvo za čišćenje i mazivo kompatibilno s vrstom lanca i brtvama.
  • Stop the engine before working on it; never clean or lubricate the chain while the engine is running or while the wheel is driving the engine.
  • After adjustment, check wheel alignment and locking devices according to the manual.

Note: a too tightly adjusted chain can damage bearings and transmission; follow the manufacturer's specification.

Brakes

4. Signs that brakes need an inspection

  • The lever or pedal suddenly feels softer, sinks, or has unusual travel.
  • You notice a leak, damaged hose, or fluid level below the allowed limit.
  • Severe scraping, vibration, pulling to one side, or noticeably weaker braking occurs.
  • The warning light stays on or the behaviour of the system has changed.

If braking behaviour has changed or a fault is suspected, do not continue riding; arrange for a professional inspection. Do not open the hydraulic system without appropriate knowledge and procedure.

Journeys

5. Preparing the motorcycle for a longer route

  • Check tyres, brakes, chain, lights, fluids, and regular service intervals.
  • Secure luggage so that it does not interfere with handling, suspension, wheels, or the exhaust system.
  • Adjust tyre pressure and load in accordance with the manual and the equipment you are carrying.
  • Check the weather forecast, roadworks, road conditions, and any restrictions from official sources.
  • Bring basic tools, documents, and a breakdown plan; for remote sections, check coverage and available assistance in advance.
How to read data

6. Specification is not the same as an actual test

When comparing vehicles, we separate three things: manufacturer data, independent test results, and owner experiences. Power, weight, and fuel consumption can be measured using different methods, so they should only be compared when the baseline is clear.

  • Check the model year and exact specification.
  • Distinguish kerb weight from dry weight.
  • For fuel consumption, state the test cycle or measurement conditions.
  • Alongside any claim of faults, state the source and sample size, rather than just an isolated anecdote.

Sources and further reading

For tyre pressures, service intervals, clearances, and procedures, the manufacturer's manual for the specific vehicle is always the primary source. For general information, the following sources are also useful:

  • Michelin Motorcycle — information on motorcycle tyres and their use.
  • Dunlop Motorcycle Tires — tyre guides and information.
  • NHTSA: Tire Safety — general tyre safety guidelines.

Editorial note: the advice is general and does not constitute a service procedure for every model. Before publishing specific values, the exact model, year, and primary source must be stated.
