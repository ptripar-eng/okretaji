From 21st place to the podium: Verstappen's masterclass, Antonelli's errors and Norris's rain-soaked victory

Norris takes the win, but Verstappen's charge from the penultimate grid slot defines the race. Interlagos saw alternating rain, dry track and Mercedes errors, which nonetheless secured them the constructors' championship.

Okretaji · 9 November 2026 · Editorial team

Max Verstappen started from 21st place and finished second. Lando Norris took victory with a 14.602-second lead. Kimi Antonelli lost the lead after a spin, George Russell also made a mistake on the slippery asphalt, and Mercedes nevertheless secured the constructors' championship right here in Brazil. Few races manage to reward and punish a team so simultaneously.

Verstappen: 21st on the grid, eighth by the fifth lap

Penalties for power unit component changes relegated Verstappen to the penultimate grid spot. On a wet track, that didn't slow him down: by the end of the first lap he was 13th, and by the fifth, eighth. Overtaking wasn't simply the result of better straight-line pace. At Interlagos, the highest-grip line changed from corner to corner, and Verstappen precisely chose his moments of attack.

On lap 38 he passed team-mate Isack Hadjar for second place. Hadjar finished third and secured a double podium for Red Bull. Verstappen couldn't catch Norris, but his fightback from the back of the pack was the day's most impressive individual charge.

Antonelli led, but turn one became his trap

Antonelli spun out during the formation lap before the race, but the car remained intact enough for the start. When the lights went out, he made a brilliant start and took the lead ahead of Norris. The gap grew while the track was damp. Then the asphalt began to dry and the race turned into a battle with tyre temperatures.

Norris switched to slick tyres on lap 16, Antonelli a lap later. McLaren's already warmed-up tyres allowed Norris to quickly close the gap. Antonelli reported unusual car behaviour over the team radio and suspected a suspension issue. At the start of lap 20 he spun at turn one and handed over the lead, then ran wide once more to lose further positions. He finished fifth, alongside a subsequent five-second penalty for an unsafe release from the pits, which did not affect his finishing position.

Why were tyres more important than raw speed?

According to Pirelli, track temperature at the start was only 16 °C. Everyone started on intermediate tyres, designed for wet conditions. As the asphalt dried, those tyres overheated and degraded rapidly. Switching to soft slick tyres was therefore inevitable, but on sections of the still-slippery track they needed to be brought into their operating temperature window quickly and carefully.

It was precisely the difference in warm-up after the initial pit stops that helped Norris catch Antonelli. However, his mistakes cannot be boiled down to tyres alone: the driver himself also reported a suspected technical issue. Russell, on the other hand, lost control exiting the pits and clipped the barrier. When the rain returned towards the end of the race, Mercedes gained better late-race pace by pitting Russell earlier for intermediate tyres, so Antonelli handed fourth place over by team order.

Norris won because he best tied together three different races

Brazil essentially offered three races in one: a wet start, a drying middle phase and a wet finish once again. After a poorer start, Norris stayed patient, nailed the crucial phase of switching to slick tyres and then controlled his lead. Even the return of rain couldn't take victory away from him. His third win of the season might not have been the most spectacular drive of the day, but it was the most complete.

A special irony for Mercedes: Antonelli squandered a chance for victory, Russell lost time through his own mistake, but fourth and fifth places were enough for the team to secure the constructors' championship. Speed wins races; points throughout the season win championships.

What remains after Interlagos?

Verstappen proved once again that starting position is not a sentence. Norris showed how much a cool head is worth when conditions change from lap to lap. And on the very same day, Mercedes received both a warning and confirmation: the car can be exceptionally fast, but tyre management and driver errors still decide victory. They lost the race in Brazil, but won the championship.
