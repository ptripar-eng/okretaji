Which tool to take on a six-day motorbike trip across Europe

A week-long trip most often brings a flat tyre, a loose bolt or a blown fuse. For that you need a small, tried-and-tested kit, not a workshop in a top case. Alongside the inventory, we also bring you the equipment that certain countries require by law.

Okretaji · Guide · 3 November 2026

Bikers who have been travelling around Europe for years rarely agree on oil brands on forums, but when it comes to tools they agree: carry what you know how to fix your bike with. Most serious breakdowns, such as electronics, the clutch or the gearbox, cannot be fixed by the roadside anyway. That is when an assistance number helps, not a bigger bag.

Tyres: breakdown number one

A punctured tyre is the most likely problem on the road and the only one that can almost always be sorted out on the spot.

  • Tubeless tyres: a plug kit (reamer, plug tool, plugs and glue) and something to inflate the tyre with.
  • Mini 12V compressor or CO₂ canisters: canisters are smaller and lighter, but run out quickly, especially on a rear tyre. A compressor takes up more space, but it does not run out.
  • Tyres with an inner tube: besides a puncture repair kit, you need tyre levers and wheel removal tools, which is a noticeably harder roadside job.
  • Pressure gauge: small and cheap, and pressure should be checked both after a repair and when the bike is loaded with luggage.
A plug is a temporary solution. Ride moderately to a tyre fitter and have the tyre checked as soon as possible.

What to put in the bag

Spanners and keys

  • Torx and Allen keys in the sizes your bike actually uses
  • Small 1/4" ratchet with sockets
  • Combination pliers or a multi-tool
  • Adjustable spanner
  • Wheel axle spanner, if the factory kit does not have one

Consumables

  • Electrical tape and duct tape
  • Cable ties of various lengths and Velcro strap
  • A bag of spare bolts, nuts and washers
  • Spare fuses
  • Work gloves and a rag

Spares and fluids

  • Bulbs, if lights are not LED
  • A little engine oil, if the bike consumes oil
  • Grease and chain spray for a longer trip; shaft drive does not require this
  • Spare motorcycle key, stored separately from the main one

For the rider

  • Small first aid kit
  • High-visibility vest within easy reach, not at the bottom of the case
  • Mobile phone charging cable from the bike
  • Assistance number (motoring club, insurance or manufacturer) also written down on paper

Test before the trip

The best kit is the one tested on your own bike. A week before departure, try using every tool from the bag to loosen what you might need on the road: wheel axles, mirrors, the battery cover, seat and luggage rack mounts. It quickly shows what is missing and what is just extra weight. The factory kit under the seat is often a good start, but rarely covers everything.

What the laws require by country

Rules for motorcycles differ from those for cars and are often more lenient. The table shows what a motorcyclist must carry according to an overview by the Austrian automobile club ÖAMTC (as of 2 November 2026), along with rules on rider gear listed by the European federation of motorcyclists FEMA.

CountryRequirement for motorcyclists
CroatiaSpare bulb kit, unless the motorcycle has xenon or LED lights. Headlights must be switched on during the day.
SloveniaFirst aid kit only for motorcycles registered in Slovenia.
AustriaFirst aid kit.
HungaryHigh-visibility vest, also for the passenger.
SlovakiaHigh-visibility vest (also for the passenger) and first aid kit.
CzechiaFirst aid kit.
SerbiaFirst aid kit.
MontenegroFirst aid kit and spare bulbs, except with xenon or LED lights.
FranceHigh-visibility vest, also for the passenger. Rider and passenger must wear CE-marked gloves, and FEMA also lists reflective helmet stickers.
Italy, Germany, SwitzerlandÖAMTC lists no special equipment carrying obligations. Daytime running lights are compulsory in Italy and Switzerland (FEMA).
Rules change, and sources sometimes differ in details. FEMA, for instance, also lists a vest for Slovenia. A vest and a small first aid kit together take up very little space, so it is worth carrying them on every trip, while checking the rules for transit countries before departure.

In short

For six days on tarmac, a tyre repair kit, a few spanners tested on your own motorcycle, a handful of consumables, a vest and a first aid kit are sufficient. Everything else is covered by roadside assistance.
