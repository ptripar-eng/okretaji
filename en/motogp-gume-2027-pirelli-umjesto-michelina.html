Pirelli is entering MotoGP

Pirelli is taking over from Michelin in MotoGP in 2027, where the tyre pressure monitoring system remains—having affected Pedro Acosta's tactics in Mandalika and cost Joan Mir a podium in Catalonia.

Okretaji · 11 November 2026

When Pedro Acosta's front tyre pressure dropped in Mandalika, he dropped back behind Marc Márquez several times to lift it above the mandatory limit in his slipstream. Since pressure will continue to be monitored next season, the question is how much Pirelli's tyre will change tactics in such situations.

Why Michelin is leaving

When the flag drops on the final race of the season in Valencia on November 29, Michelin's 11-season run in the premier class will come to an end, with Pirelli supplying tyres exclusively from 2027 under a five-year plan running until the end of 2031. Pirelli already supplies Moto2 and Moto3, and its initial announcement also covered MotoE, which was put on hold after the 2025 season.

While the organiser sought a single supplier for multiple classes, Michelin's Piero Taramasso explained that the company wants to focus on a smaller number of championships to provide better support to teams and riders. At the same time, Michelin wanted to remain in the premier class, for which it had already begun developing compounds tailored to the new generation of motorcycles.

When pressure changes the order

According to the monitoring system introduced by MotoGP in 2023, the reference minimum for the 2026 season is 1.80 bar at the front and 1.68 bar at the rear. Riders must maintain the prescribed pressure during at least 60% of Grand Prix laps and 30% of Sprint laps, and breaching this rule carries a 16-second penalty in the main race or eight in the sprint.

When Joan Mir dropped from second to 13th in Catalonia following a penalty, he lost a podium in the same way as Maverick Viñales in Qatar 2025. Tyre pressure issues can even be felt before the finish line, as shown by Jorge Martín, who finished the Mandalika sprint in fourth.

FIM has confirmed that the monitoring system will continue in 2027, though its decision did not specify the numerical thresholds for the new tyre. Pirelli motorcycle racing director Giorgio Barbier said the company is counting on the rule continuing, with the possibility of later changes.

Michelin's front tyre that never made it to the races

Michelin tested a new front tyre at Misano in 2024 designed to mitigate the pressure issue, and according to Crash.net, teams rode on it for about half an hour before its introduction was postponed and later cancelled. Former crew chief Ramón Forcada said that, based on his conversation with Pirelli, the company was considering pre-race checks to avoid retroactive penalties. What teams have already tried

A development front tyre prototype featuring grooves on one side was spotted at Honda's Sepang test, which, according to a report by The Race, served to heat up faster. The Race — Honda test

At the Red Bull Ring on September 21, 18 riders from all five manufacturers rode, testing soft, medium, and hard compound slicks on 850 cm³ prototypes and modified 1000 cm³ motorcycles, according to Pirelli. Pirelli published a list of riders and manufacturers, and for a speed comparison, we would need comparable conditions and lap times. Pirelli's test report

In its final season, Michelin simplified front tyre allocation so that riders received two specifications of seven units each at most weekends, while three specifications of five units each were retained at selected circuits. Crash.net — 2026 tyre allocation

What to watch for at the first test

As the new tyres arrive alongside 850 cm³ engines, reduced aerodynamics, and a ban on ride-height devices, teams will have to figure out how much of the different braking feel comes from the tyre and how much from the motorcycle and its setup. Experience with Pirelli from Moto2 may help with adaptation, although tests alone will show how much that experience translates to a different motorcycle. FIM — 2027 technical regulations

At the first official test of the new generation, scheduled for December 1 in Valencia, it will be worth listening to what riders say about front tyre feel under braking and in dirty air. If Pirelli's tyre stays in its operating window more easily under those conditions, teams could have fewer pressure issues even with monitoring continuing. MotoGP — test schedule
