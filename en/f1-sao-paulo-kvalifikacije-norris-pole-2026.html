Norris on pole position in Brazil: rain, red flags and penalties shake up the starting grid

Norris was 0.204 seconds faster than Russell in wet qualifying. Antonelli is third, and Verstappen missed out on Q2 due to a penalty.

Okretaji · 8 November 2026 · Editorial Office

Lando Norris claimed pole position for the São Paulo Grand Prix, setting a lap time of 1:27.470 on a wet Interlagos circuit. George Russell went second-fastest, 0.204 seconds adrift, with Kimi Antonelli third, 0.625 seconds back.

Qualifying was delayed due to heavy rain, and two red flag stoppages made timing track excursions even trickier. Norris capitalised on his initial runs in Q3 before the rain intensified. This marks his third consecutive pole position at Interlagos.

Russell second in qualifying, but not on the grid

Russell received a five-place grid penalty, Leclerc ten, while Verstappen and Hamilton also face hefty penalties for power unit element changes. According to the FIA's provisional starting grid, Antonelli moves up to the front row, and Lawson to third place. Therefore, the qualifying result should not be confused with the starting grid.

Lewis Hamilton dropped out in Q2 in eleventh, and Oscar Piastri finished twelfth. Rafael Câmara triggered a red flag after going off track, whilst home hero Gabriel Bortoleto failed to make it out of Q1.

What to expect from the race?

Unpredictable weather at Interlagos and a flurry of penalties pave the way for major charges through the field. It will be especially fascinating to follow the progress of the drivers starting from the back of the grid and whether Norris can convert pole position into victory.

At Interlagos, Norris got the job done in a single lap. In the race, however, alongside speed, factors such as traffic, strategy and the weather—which can change here in a matter of minutes—will also play a decisive role.
